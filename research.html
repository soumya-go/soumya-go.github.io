Research

Research Vision

My research explores the physical and chemical processes that shape planetary atmospheres across the Universe. By combining radiative transfer, atmospheric chemistry, planetary evolution, and numerical modeling, I seek to understand how exoplanets form, evolve, and interact with their host stars. A central goal of my work is to interpret atmospheric observations and identify the signatures of clouds, chemistry, circulation, and radiation in exoplanet spectra.

Exoplanet Atmospheres

Planetary atmospheres preserve important clues about a planet's formation and evolution. My work focuses on atmospheric characterization through transmission spectroscopy, emission spectroscopy, and theoretical modeling of atmospheric structure and composition.

Hot Jupiter Physics & Chemistry

Hot Jupiters provide natural laboratories for studying atmospheric dynamics, cloud formation, chemistry, and radiative processes under extreme stellar irradiation. My research investigates heat redistribution, atmospheric circulation, and observable spectral signatures.

Radiative Transfer & Polarization

Radiative transfer provides the connection between planetary atmospheres and observations. I develop analytical and numerical models to study multiple scattering, polarization, thermal emission, and atmospheric energy transport.
